What Is Encryption Software? How It Works and Why You Need It

Whether you are securing business assets for compliance or protecting personal files from unauthorized access, understanding the mechanics of encryption is your first line of defense. This guide breaks down the science of scrambling data into plain English.

Editorial Team Updated: May 2026 16 min read
Plain-English Definition

Encryption software is a security program that uses complex mathematical algorithms to scramble plain text or files into an unreadable format called ciphertext. Only users who possess the correct cryptographic key or password can decrypt and read the original data, ensuring privacy even if the files are stolen or intercepted.

What Does Encryption Software Do?

At its core, encryption software performs a singular function: it scrambles data so that it is not recognized by unauthorized users.

When you save a document normally, it sits on your hard drive as plain text. Anyone with physical access to your drive—or malware that infiltrates your system—can read it. Encryption software sits between your data and the storage drive, applying an algorithm that turns that plain text into seemingly random characters.

The Key Risk Fact

Over 60% of data breaches involve unencrypted files left on lost hardware, misconfigured cloud servers, or stolen laptops. Password-protecting a computer login does not encrypt the files on the hard drive; a thief can simply remove the drive and read the data on another machine unless encryption software is applied.

How Modern Encryption Algorithms Work

The strength of any data encryption software relies entirely on the algorithm it uses. The industry standard today is AES (Advanced Encryption Standard).

AES-256 Encryption: What It Is and Why It Matters

AES-256 uses a 256-bit key to encrypt and decrypt data. A 256-bit key means there are 2256 possible combinations. To put that in perspective, cracking this algorithm using brute force with current supercomputers would take billions of years.

This is why governments, financial institutions, and the military use AES-256 for securing top-secret information.

Behind the Scenes: The Command Line

While user-friendly software handles this invisibly via a graphical interface, the underlying operation relies on cryptographic libraries. Here is an example of what an AES-256 encryption command looks like using OpenSSL:

openssl enc -aes-256-cbc -salt -in confidential.pdf -out confidential.pdf.enc -k YourSecurePassword123

Running this command scrambles the file, outputting a `.enc` file that cannot be opened without reversing the command with the exact password.

Symmetric vs Asymmetric Encryption — What You Need to Know

There are two primary frameworks that encryption software utilizes depending on the use case:

  • Symmetric Encryption: Uses a single key to both encrypt and decrypt the data. This is extremely fast and is the standard for data at rest (like encrypting files on your local hard drive or USB drive).
  • Asymmetric Encryption: Uses two mathematically linked keys—a public key to encrypt the data, and a private key to decrypt it. This is primarily used for data in transit (like secure email encryption software or web browsing via HTTPS/SSL).

Types of Encryption Software

Not all encryption is applied identically. Depending on what you are trying to protect, software generally falls into three categories.

File and Folder Encryption

What it does: Encrypts individual files or specific folders.

Best for: Users who want to protect specific sensitive documents, tax returns, or business records without encrypting their entire computer. Ideal for cloud storage.

Full Disk Encryption (FDE)

What it does: Encrypts the entire hard drive, including the operating system and hidden system files.

Best for: Securing laptops that might be lost or stolen. Windows BitLocker and Mac FileVault are common examples.

Email Encryption Software

What it does: Secures messages in transit so they cannot be intercepted by third parties.

Best for: Accountants, legal professionals, and healthcare providers sharing confidential data with clients.

Extending Protection: Mobile Devices and Portable Media

While traditional data protection focuses on desktop computers, modern security risks often lie in devices we carry. A comprehensive security strategy requires protecting data across multiple platforms.

Securing USB Drives and External Hard Drives

Flash drives and external hard disks are incredibly easy to misplace. Top-tier encryption tools allow users to build portable, self-contained lockers directly onto USB drives. This ensures that if the removable media is left on a train or stolen from a bag, the data within the portable locker remains entirely inaccessible to the finder.

Mobile Privacy: iOS and Android

Modern smartphones act as miniature computers, holding massive amounts of personal and corporate data. Specialized mobile security apps go beyond standard lock screens by providing:

  • Secure Media Vaults: Dedicated, encrypted spaces to isolate sensitive photos, videos, and audio files from the device's native gallery.
  • Digital Wallets & Secret Notes: Encrypted containers specifically designed for storing credit card information, PINs, and confidential memos.
  • Intrusion Monitoring: Advanced features that track and record unauthorized attempts to guess the application password.
  • Secure Browsing: In-app browsers that prevent tracking and do not retain history outside of the encrypted environment.

Evaluating Free vs. Premium Encryption Tools

When selecting a security solution, users often have to decide between free software, basic utilities, and premium, full-featured suites. Understanding the limitations is crucial—especially for business environments.

Basic & Free Utilities

Many basic tools offer simple folder-hiding mechanisms. While these are excellent for keeping files out of sight from family members on a shared home computer, they do not utilize AES algorithms to scramble the underlying data. Freemium encryption tools might provide true cryptography, but often impose severe limits, such as a maximum locker size of 1GB, restrictions on how many devices you can synchronize, or a lack of secure file-shredding capabilities.

Premium & Enterprise Solutions

For scenarios where unauthorized access could result in a data leak or compliance violation, premium software removes these limitations. A commercial license typically provides unlimited storage capacity for encrypted lockers and allows users to sync their protected data across multiple desktop and mobile devices simultaneously.

Furthermore, premium applications offer secure sharing features, allowing business leaders and managers to grant temporary or restricted access to encrypted company servers without exposing the master credentials to employees.

Encryption Compliance Requirements for Businesses

If your organization handles confidential documents, consumer data, or healthcare records, encryption is not just a best practice—it is a legal requirement.

HIPAA (Healthcare)

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act requires covered entities to implement mechanisms that encrypt and decrypt electronic protected health information (ePHI). AES-256 data encryption software satisfies this requirement for data at rest.

GDPR (Data Privacy)

Article 32 of the GDPR explicitly mentions the "pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data" as an appropriate technical measure to ensure data security. Failure to encrypt breached data can result in massive fines.

PCI-DSS (Finance)

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard mandates strong cryptography and security protocols to safeguard primary account numbers (PAN) during storage and transmission.

Owner-Safe Troubleshooting & Password Recovery

We receive many queries asking things like, "How to decrypt an AES-256 file without the password" or "Forgot encryption software password."

The hard truth: True encryption software does not feature "backdoors." If you lose your master password and have no backup keys, the data is gone. This is by design to ensure hackers cannot bypass the security.

Best Practices for Key Management

  • Use a Password Manager: Store your encryption passwords in a secure, audited password manager.
  • Save Recovery Keys: When setting up tools like BitLocker or Veracrypt, print the generated recovery key and store it in a physical safe.
  • Beware of Recovery Scams: Software that claims it can break AES-256 encryption is either a scam or malware. Legitimate recovery only works if a flaw existed in the software's implementation, not the algorithm itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common queries regarding data encryption tools and techniques.

  • What is the difference between symmetric and asymmetric encryption?

    Symmetric uses one key to both lock and unlock data (best for files). Asymmetric uses a public key to lock and a private key to unlock (best for sharing data over the internet, like email).

  • Does encryption slow down my PC significantly?

    No. Modern CPUs have built-in hardware acceleration for encryption algorithms like AES. You will likely notice zero performance difference during daily use.

  • Can encrypted files be hacked?

    The AES-256 algorithm itself has never been cracked. However, hackers can steal your password via phishing, keyloggers, or guessing if you use a weak password. The vulnerability is always human error, not the math.

  • What happens when you encrypt a file?

    The software reads the file's binary data and runs it through a complex mathematical formula, resulting in a new file containing randomized characters. The original plain text is overwritten or securely deleted.

  • Do I need encryption software if I have a firewall?

    Yes. A firewall protects your network from outside intrusion. Encryption protects your actual files if the firewall is breached or if someone physically steals your device.

  • Who provides the most reliable disk encryption software?

    For native OS integration, Microsoft (BitLocker) and Apple (FileVault) are highly reliable. For cross-platform, granular file control, and portable USB encryption, third-party developers like NewSoftwares.net (Folder Lock) provide robust, independently tested solutions.
